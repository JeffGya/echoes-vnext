# Genre Heuristics

Use these as working heuristics, not rigid formulas.

## Social Sim and Life Sim

- Character expression must be visible in routines, choices, reactions, conflicts, and preferences.
- Relationship systems need friction and surprise. Purely positive progress bars flatten drama.
- Spaces matter. Rooms, objects, schedules, and proximity should create stories, not just house stats.
- Repetition is acceptable only if new emotional context or new consequences keep it alive.
- Players should feel like caretakers, meddlers, matchmakers, or observers with influence, not spreadsheet operators.

## Tomodachi-Like Character Drama

- The appeal comes from low-stakes absurdity, emergent social collisions, and recognizable personalities.
- Strong payoff usually comes from anticipation plus payoff: who likes who, who clashes, who surprises you.
- Characters need strong readable differentiation fast. If everyone feels tonally similar, social comedy dies.
- Interventions should be light-touch but meaningful. Too much control kills spontaneity; too little kills investment.

## Autobattler with RPG Elements

- Pre-battle decisions must generate readable mid-battle outcomes.
- Positioning, economy, traits, loadouts, class identity, and progression should create learnable cause and effect.
- RPG progression should widen expressive builds, not bury the player in opaque percentages.
- If combat is automatic, the player still needs decision windows before, between, or around combat.
- Spectacle matters, but unreadable spectacle kills mastery.

## Character Attachment in System-Driven Games

- Attachment often comes from a mix of competence, vulnerability, memory, and consequence.
- A character becomes memorable when their behavior changes outcomes the player can feel.
- Writing helps, but attachment usually fails when behavior, UI, and consequences do not support it.

## UI as Gameplay

- Use hierarchy to tell the player what matters now, what is changing, and why.
- Emotional state should be readable without burying the player in meters.
- Tactical state should be interpretable at a glance and explainable after the fact.
- Flavor should not conceal decision-critical information.
