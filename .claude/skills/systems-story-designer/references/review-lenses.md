# Review Lenses

Use only the lenses that materially affect the request. Do not score everything by default.

## Core Lenses

### Player Fantasy

- What fantasy is the player supposed to inhabit?
- Is that fantasy visible in actions, feedback, and pacing, or only in the pitch text?
- Does the game ask the player to feel something it does not structurally support?

### Attachment Drivers

- Why should the player care about these characters?
- Do characters show competence, vulnerability, need, unpredictability, taste, or memory through play?
- Are bonds mechanically meaningful, or only cosmetic?

### Decision Clarity

- What decisions does the player make per minute, per session, and per run?
- Which choices are meaningful, reversible, or high-stakes?
- Are outcomes legible enough that the player can improve?

### System Interplay

- Do economy, progression, social systems, battle systems, and presentation reinforce each other?
- Are there dead systems that do not meaningfully affect the rest of the game?
- Are there loops that collapse into obvious dominant play?

### UI and Feedback

- Does the interface explain what matters right now?
- Are priority, urgency, mood, intent, and consequence visible?
- Does the UI heighten the fantasy while remaining readable?

### Emotional Pacing

- Where do curiosity, tension, relief, surprise, care, and loss appear?
- Does the game vary emotional temperature, or stay flat?
- Are key emotional beats earned through play?

### Thematic Reinforcement

- Do mechanics, feedback, and structure express the theme, or just the writing?
- Does the setting change how the game feels and behaves, or only how it is named?

### Retention Ethic

- Does the design create desire to return through mastery, attachment, discovery, and consequence?
- Is the retention strategy fair, or is it leaning on friction, anxiety, or obligation?

## Response Shape

When reviewing, lead with the 3 to 5 highest-impact findings. For each finding:

- what is failing
- why it hurts the player experience
- what change would improve it

Use severity language when useful: critical, major, moderate, minor.
