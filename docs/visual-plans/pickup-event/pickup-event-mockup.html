Design notes: what each concept proposes, what to judge, risks

All text, names and barks in this file are written by the agents as a proposal. Jeff decides the wording.

Concept A: Walk the field (combat board, end of combat)

The loot lies where the enemies fell. One Echo steps out of the line and walks to the finds. It picks up some and leaves some. The fight board is the place for the loot.

Concept C: The circle (path view, after an encounter)

The party token opens into its Echoes. They stand in a ring around the finds. One Echo picks. A second Echo (the bystander) steps toward a find that was left and says one line. The bystander shows a second Echo's taste in every event.

What to judge in motion

  • Is the chooser clear at a glance? Does the Echo that steps out read as "the one who decides"?
  • Can you tell the six reasons apart with the sound off? The closest pair is Froze and Saw its worth. The second closest pair is Likes the kind and Went bold. Use the "six reasons" button, then the 0.5x speed.
  • Does the left find read as "visible but not mine"? Compare Swap offered (85 percent, pulse, mark) with Swap spent (60 percent, quiet).
  • Does the swap feel like a trade? Try a drag, a two-tap trade, and the refused drop. The swap never confirms by itself.
  • Is Skip enough? Is Continue a chore? Look at the Short and Brief forms (events 5 to 12 and 13 and later).
  • A: does a clustered field and a scattered field both read? C: do 2 and 5 Echoes both read? Does the bystander line pull attention from the pick?
  • Does it feel like a game, or like a web page? Turn on Grayscale and check that the finds still read.

Risks

  • The Echo is a disc with a letter. It has no eyes or hands. Froze and Saw its worth read only in time (a stop, a shake, a glint). In a still frame they look alike.
  • The finds are flat plates. If the slab edge, the tilt and the shadow are cut, they turn into cards on a field.
  • Marks are small at 1280 x 720. Pips are about 7 px. Check with the 1920 x 1080 board.
  • Token blue on the floor is 1.09:1 and the token outline is 2.28:1. Both fail 3:1. The white letter carries identity. This comes from the combat mockup.
  • The left plate (#CDB791) is 3.17:1 on the floor. It reads, but it is quiet. #E0CDAE (3.97:1) is the fallback.
  • Two carried finds sit 48 units apart. Targets of 48 units touch. The rule asks for 8 units between targets. Turn on "Show frame guides" to see it.
  • C needs new drawing logic: the party token opens into single Echo tokens. The ring is a new layout the player did not see in combat.
  • C crowding: the finds in the middle are close together. Check them at the smallest window.
  • Motion limits: this page cannot show the real frame budget of the game. Test animation cost in Godot at the minimum window.

What this page does not claim

  • Which Echo is the chooser, the reason, the picks and the bystander come from invented data here. The game must supply them.
  • The thresholds for the full, short and brief forms (events 1 to 4, 5 to 12, 13 and later) are proposals.
  • No audio plays. The sound cue line is only a review aid.
  • "Resolve opens here" is a stand-in. It is not a design.
