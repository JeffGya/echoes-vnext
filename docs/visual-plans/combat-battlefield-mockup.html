E. Battlefield rules and design principles

These rules define what the examples mean. The motion and art treatment are proposals for later stories.

Game rules · sourced

  1. Combat is automatic. The Echo decides its movement and action. A lit destination shows a resolved move; it does not ask the player to choose a tile.
  2. Only walkable cells are floor. Void has no tile. The bridge and hazard examples use named test fixtures; they are not a promise that every generated map has this layout.
  3. Unstable resolves one entry. It prefers the straight outward cell. A sole legal forward diagonal can receive the actor; tied legal diagonals use the fixture fallback and cause damage.
  4. Binding stops movement on entry. It does not deal damage or add a lasting state in this example.
  5. Burning resolves at activation end. It checks the final occupied cell after the actor's primary action, once for that activation.
  6. HP changes match the actor and card. A loss uses a red signed number and visible recoil. It is one outcome shown in two places.

Presentation principles · proposed

  1. Keep the map true. Draw material and reactions inside their cells. Do not shift the grid or imply new obstacles.
  2. Show one movement intention. Light the voluntary destination when the actor gathers. Hold it through continuous travel, then fade it on arrival; clear destination and route when a hazard interrupts the move.
  3. Separate chosen motion from forced motion. All voluntary moves share one gather and continuous travel. A slip interrupts that rhythm and adds no second destination marker.
  4. Give each cue one job. Ground shows footing, the active ring shows whose turn it is, the target bracket shows a short target relation, and actor plus card show the result.
  5. Yield under overlap. Keep active actor and target readable first. Let optional path, spirit and detail marks soften when they meet.
  6. Make motion optional. Rest and reduced-motion views show the resolved state. Colour also needs a shape, label or visible consequence.
