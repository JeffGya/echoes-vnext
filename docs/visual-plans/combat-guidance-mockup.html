World-based slowdown cue · game and cultural references

The cue is a shared breeze: irregular dust and a few dry leaf flecks cross the floor while two peripheral grass tufts sway. These exist before aiming and keep the same marks, colour and direction; their cadence slows with the actors, then eases back on confirmation or cancellation. There is no slowdown caption, actor aura, new targeting ring or screen tint.

Ghost of Tsushima’s developer VFX account describes coherent wind across particles, foliage, cloth and ropes. That supports this environmental communication approach; it is not a claim that Ghost uses our slowdown cue. Square Enix’s FFVII Remake system guide documents slower time while its command menu is open; its direct-command system is a timing comparison.

The material study includes Akan Adinkra stamp-making with ink and comb-drawn borders, Ghanaian Kente strip weaving, and the distinct Bamana bogolan tradition in Mali. Their designs carry specific meanings; none is being labelled as a time symbol. The cue uses original, non-symbolic hand-inked environmental marks within Echoes’ existing clay palette.

Realm-specific environmental cues · locked principle

The shared rule is the change in cadence: existing world motion slows when guidance opens and recovers when it closes. Each Realm supplies its own materials, palette and atmosphere; dust and grass are this mockup’s example, not mandatory scenery for every Realm.

  • Dry landscapes: dust, dry leaves and grass.
  • Wooded landscapes: falling leaves, foliage and light mist.
  • River landscapes: reeds, floating debris and surface ripples.
  • Snow landscapes: windblown snow and exposed vegetation.

These are candidate adaptations, not newly locked biome assets. A deliberately still Realm needs a fitting visible motion anchor, such as an existing hanging fibre or cloth, to make the slowdown legible.

Validate contrast, density and visibility against each Realm’s artwork and camera framing. Preserve clear actors, HP and targeting; this single-field mockup does not establish that all Realm variants have passed visual testing.

Review by opening and closing Guide without reading the HUD. The whole field should appear to lose and regain tempo, while targets, HP, camera and pointer remain clear. Reduced motion retains static scenery without particle drift.

Unselected timing comparisons · reference only

Locked direction: slow while aiming. Echo autonomy does not require fast clicking. Slowing the battle may give players time to read and place guidance while preserving visible continuity. Full pause removes that time pressure completely, but changes the current GDD’s automatic-only rule; Live and Pause remain comparison tools, not adopted alternatives; this visual approval does not change canon.

The Live / Slow / Pause buttons are inside the guidance panel. Switching them keeps your current battle, target, charge and pending guidance. Use Restart battle when you want to compare a fresh attempt. Live continues normally. Slow uses one-quarter playback speed while aiming. Pause finishes the current action and waits at its boundary; confirming or cancelling restores the selected playback speed. If guidance is already pending, close the panel with Back to battle to let the Echoes answer. The one-quarter factor is a study starting value.

Compare long reading, a moving recipient, cancellation and a request made near a round boundary. Prefer the least interruption that lets the player explain what they sent and to whom, without rushed mistakes.
