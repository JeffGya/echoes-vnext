# Competitor Analysis: Hades
## Indirect Competitor Assessment (Design Lessons)

**Game:** Hades  
**Developer:** Supergiant Games  
**Platforms:** Nintendo Switch (2020), PC, PS4, Xbox, macOS, iOS  
**Release:** 2020  
**Market Position:** Indie action-game cultural phenomenon  

---

## Business Performance

**Revenue (Estimated):**
- Lifetime sales: 3M+ copies
- Estimated lifetime revenue: $30M+
- Annual revenue: $4-6M (tail revenue)
- Platform: 40% Switch, 35% PC, 15% console, 10% mobile

**Player Base:**
- Total players: 3M+
- Twitch streams: 50K+ weekly (still, 5+ years later)
- Community subreddit: 350K+ subscribers
- Retention: 25% long-term (action games typically 15-20%)

---

## Why Hades Matters for Echoes

**Hades is NOT a direct competitor** (action game vs strategy), but represents indie game design excellence worth studying.

### Key Lessons for Echoes vNext

**1. Narrative > Graphics**
- Hades: Voice acting, character depth, mythology authenticity
- Budget: $4M (indie budget, not AAA)
- Impact: Narrative became primary draw
- Lesson for Echoes: Professional writing/localization is investment worth making

**2. Accessibility Design**
- Hades: Assist mode + difficulty options from day 1
- No gatekeeping narrative behind mechanical skill
- 40% of players use assist mode
- Lesson for Echoes: Design for accessibility without compromise (story difficulty option critical)

**3. Cultural Authenticity**
- Hades: Greek mythology researched, respected, not exoticized
- Creative liberty taken respectfully
- Player recognition of authenticity drives engagement
- Lesson for Echoes: Akan cultural grounding must feel authentic (not cosmetic)

**4. Cross-Platform Excellence**
- Hades: Same experience on all platforms
- Mobile version not gimped
- Switch version preferred (portability factor)
- Lesson for Echoes: Mobile-first design, port to console, not vice versa

**5. Roguelike Structure**
- Hades: Permadeath + progression combined
- Each run is contained (30-40 min)
- Meta-progression sustains engagement
- Lesson for Echoes: Campaign persistence is different design (but equally valid)

**6. Relationship Depth in Action Game**
- Hades: Character relationships matter mechanically and emotionally
- Dialogues replay-able multiple times
- Relationship progression visible/earnable
- Lesson for Echoes: Relationships should affect gameplay, not just story

**7. Post-Launch Support**
- Hades: Regular updates for 2+ years after launch
- Community feels heard and engaged
- Updates added content, not gacha mechanics
- Lesson for Echoes: Plan for 24+ month support cycle

## Market Lessons (Not Direct Competition)

**Why Hades Succeeds (across genres):**
1. Exceptional artistic direction (visual + audio)
2. Professional writing + voice acting
3. Respect for source material (Greek mythology)
4. Accessibility without compromise
5. Community engagement + listening
6. Post-launch support meaningful
7. Cultural appeal broad (mythology universal)
8. Mechanical excellence underneath

**Echoes Should Replicate (where applicable):**
- ✓ Professional writing + voice acting (diaspora languages?)
- ✓ Respect for Akan cultural sources
- ✓ Accessibility by design (difficulty options)
- ✓ Community engagement from day 1
- ✓ Post-launch support commitment
- ✓ Mechanical excellence (determinism)
- ✓ Artistic vision strong (West African aesthetic)
- ✓ Cultural appeal authentic (not exoticized)

---

## Risk Areas (Where Echoes Could Differ)

**Hades Advantages Echoes May Not Replicate:**
1. AAA-level art direction ($4M indie budget is exceptional)
2. Supergiant's brand reputation (established pedigree)
3. Nintendo partnership/optimization
4. Soundtrack prestige (Darren Korb renown)
5. Action game accessibility (lower skill barrier for "feel good")

**Mitigation:**
- Echoes should not try to out-Hades Hades
- Own different aesthetic (West African, not Greek)
- Own different mechanical angle (strategy, not action)
- Compete on narrative + culture authenticity, not budget

---

## Revenue Benchmark

**Hades shows premium indie pricing works:**
- Launch: $24.99 (console market)
- 3M copies = $75M gross (at $25 average)
- Lifetime: $30M+ net (estimated)

**Echoes Pricing Implication:**
- Different genre (lower price point justified)
- Mobile platform (lower price point expected)
- Same premium indie audience likely
- Realistic target: $7-12 average ARPU

| Accessibility | Assist mode, difficulty | Difficulty + story options |
| Cultural respect | Greek mythology authentic | Akan culture authentic |
| Cross-platform | Switch optimized | Mobile optimized |
| Relationships | Meaningful + repeatable | Core + persistent |
| Post-launch | 2+ years updates planned | 24+ month support planned |

## Conclusion for Echoes

**Hades is a masterclass in indie game execution**, not a direct competitor. Study its approach to:
- Cultural authenticity (Echoes doing this already)
- Accessibility design (Echoes should prioritize)
- Cross-platform excellence (Echoes targeting this)
- Relationship mechanics (Echoes core mechanic)
- Community engagement (Echoes planned)

**Revenue benchmark:** Hades proves $30M+ is achievable for top indie games. Echoes' realistic target: $5-15M lifetime (similar audience size, different price point/platform mix).

**Confidence in differentiation:** HIGH. Genre difference is fundamental; both can succeed in market simultaneously.
