# Echoes of the Sankofa — Calling Visual and Equipment Bible

**Status:** First grounded direction for concept development  
**Scope:** Uncalled plus the six Standing-3 Callings  
**Primary design canon:** `docs/Echoes vNext Working GDD.md`  
**Related references:** `docs/calling-reference.md`, `docs/art-direction.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how each Calling should read as a lived identity across:

- character silhouette and posture;
- combat behavior and movement;
- garment lineage;
- armor and gear construction;
- weapon expression and soft Calling bias;
- charm language;
- color, material, and wear;
- incomplete and recovering Echo identity.

It is not an equipment-lock table. **Every Calling can equip every weapon.** The GDD establishes that Calling-and-weapon fit is a soft bias: fit may improve fluency, behavior, performance, and expression, but it never determines eligibility.

The visual goal is therefore:

> A Calling should remain legible when clothing, weapon, and charm are mixed, without turning the Echo into a generic RPG class costume.

Calling and weapon are separate axes:

- **Calling** defines why and how the Echo acts: posture, decision tendency, emotional expression, garment silhouette, and protective emphasis.
- **Weapon** defines what actions become available: reach, arc, tempo, target relationship, and tactical opportunity.
- **Bias** defines how naturally the two axes reinforce one another.
- **The player may combine them freely.** An unusual combination should create interesting expression, not an invalid build.

## 3. Cross-Calling Visual Rules

### 3.1 People before loadouts

- The Echo must read as a person before reading as a combat role.
- Calling expression sits on top of age, body, gender expression, emotion, relationships, and recovered identity.
- Equipment supports identity; it does not replace it.
- A mixed loadout should look chosen, inherited, traded, repaired, or adapted—not procedurally assembled.

### 3.2 Incomplete identity

Incomplete Echoes use localized fields of identity color in place of settled skin, with loose Threads at their boundaries. These areas are not holes, wounds, glowing cloth, or missing artwork.

- Color indicates current emotional state before it indicates Calling.
- Loose Threads settle through rites, lived experience, and recovered stories.
- Calling color must therefore never overwrite emotion color.
- Higher completion means fewer loose ends, more continuous edges, and more confident material contact—not more visual effects.

### 3.3 Garment and gear relationship

The base garment and protective gear should be designed as one system.

- Reinforcement follows seams, hems, shoulder structures, waist wraps, and existing garment panels.
- Added protection uses quilted cotton, layered strip-woven cloth, leather, basketry, wood, and restrained brass fittings.
- Avoid placing a generic fantasy breastplate over a culturally specific garment.
- Cloth remains visible enough that the Calling lineage survives at combat distance.
- Armor distribution communicates behavior: where an Echo expects contact, carries weight, or preserves mobility.

### 3.4 Cultural grounding

- Use documented garment construction and textile rhythms as reference, not a collage of “African” motifs.
- Kente is narrow-strip woven cloth assembled from planned strips; its overall rhythm is structured rather than random.
- Classic prestige ntoma/ntama is a continuous, voluminous drape. Combat adaptation must still read as one cloth, securely managed around an underlayer.
- Fugu/batakari is a constructed, strip-woven smock with broad shoulder and flared-body potential. It should not become a printed T-shirt.
- Kaba and slit is a Ghanaian tailored women’s ensemble. A masculine or unisex parallel should share its formal tailoring logic rather than be described as a historically exact “male kaba.”
- Wax print may appear where appropriate, but it should not substitute for all Ghanaian textile identities.
- Never invent Adinkra-like symbols. Confirmed symbols may only appear where their actual meaning is relevant.

### 3.5 Color and light

Global foundation — named by pigment and dye, not by hex code, so palette choices stay grounded in how a real material behaves rather than an arbitrary swatch:

- **forest green** — indigo overdyed with a plant-based yellow, the traditional route to green in West African cloth dyeing rather than a single "green dye";
- **warm ivory** — undyed, handspun cotton in its natural cream state;
- **Akan gold** — actual gold leaf or gold dust (sika), not a yellow pigment standing in for it;
- **terracotta** — camwood (osun) powder, the red-brown heartwood pigment used across Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, and Liberia as body paint and cloth dye; in Ghana it specifically marked warriors after combat;
- **deep indigo-black** — cloth dipped in indigo dye twenty-plus times, the traditional route to a true blue-black;
- **soft blue** — the same indigo dye family at one or two dips only: a pale, early-stage blue, not a separate pigment.

These describe real material behavior, not settled ritual meaning — see §14 before assigning specific cultural meaning to any of them.

New colors beyond this foundation are expected and encouraged: Callings need enough palette range to stay visually distinct from each other. The rule is precision, not restriction — every color named anywhere in this document, foundation or Calling-specific, should be a specific, locatable pigment, dye, or material, never a vague mood word like "dark brown ink."

Callings use **palette bias**, not uniform color coding. Material, cut, posture, and action profile must carry more recognition than hue.

### 3.6 Weapon-slot rule

“Weapon” is a mechanical category, not a synonym for anything held in the hand. An object belongs in the weapon slot only when it creates a concrete combat action with a readable target, range or area, effect, timing, animation, and counterplay.

The slot may contain:

- a physical battlefield weapon;
- a defensive weapon set that attacks, guards, intercepts, or counters;
- a hunting weapon adapted to combat;
- a practical striking or cutting tool adapted to combat;
- a spiritual or story-power conduit that directly damages, marks, binds, interrupts, exposes, or otherwise contests an enemy.

An office staff, state sword, prestige flywhisk, bell, bead strand, vessel, or similar object is **not** a weapon merely because it can be held dramatically. If it lacks an authored combat action, it belongs under relic, charm, consumable, regalia, or story item instead.

## 4. Weapon Research Ruling

### 4.1 Admission test: what counts as a weapon

A proposed weapon must answer all of these questions before it enters the combat catalogue:

1. What combat action does it add?
2. What can it target, and at what range or area?
3. Does it deal physical or story damage, or create a combat-relevant state such as guard, mark, bind, expose, interrupt, stagger, or displacement?
4. What is its readable anticipation, contact/effect moment, and recovery?
5. What resource, timing, cooldown, positioning demand, or other constraint prevents free use?
6. How does an enemy or player recognize and respond to it?
7. What does AI behavior try to accomplish when choosing it?

If those answers do not exist, the object is not yet a weapon. Historical importance, ceremonial authority, beauty, and symbolic charge are insufficient on their own.

### 4.2 Combat-grounded vocabulary

Historical Gold Coast warfare supports swords, lances or spears, bows, and shields as a foundational vocabulary. Wider West African collections add combat and throwing spears, arm daggers, palm-loop knives, curved savannah swords, straight takouba-type swords, striking clubs, hide shields, and some functional throwing blades. These broaden the game beyond a Western-fantasy armoury without claiming that every form is Akan.

The game should label three levels of grounding:

- **Akan/Gold Coast grounded:** a combat category is documented in the region, though the exact game object may be adapted.
- **Wider West African grounded:** the form comes from a named neighbouring cultural or regional lineage and retains that attribution.
- **World-original:** the mechanic and silhouette are invented for *Echoes of the Sankofa*, even when built from regionally plausible materials.

Do not flatten Yoruba, Hausa, Fulbe, Soninke, Manding, Tiv, Tuareg, Dagomba, or other lineages into a generic “African weapon” label.

### 4.3 Combat weapon families

Weapon families are authored independently of Callings. “Current” means the present adjacent-melee/guard combat surface can represent the basic action; reach, ranged, area, mark, and other advanced expressions require later implementation.

| Weapon family | Combat action profile | Visual/action read | Grounding | System status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Short sword / combat blade** | quick adjacent cut, counter, finish | compact one-handed arc; free off-hand | Gold Coast category; practical `uda` evidence in wider West Africa | basic melee current; counters future |
| **Long sword / sabre** | committed cut, pressure, eventual cleave | long diagonal or horizontal arc | Manding curved sword and takouba-type lineages | basic melee current; cleave future |
| **Knife / arm dagger** | fast close strike, disengage, opportunistic finish | tight line close to torso | Hausa arm dagger and Tiv palm-loop knife lineages | basic melee current; mobility effects future |
| **Spear / lance** | thrust, brace, line control, eventual reach | long straight line with functional iron head | Gold Coast and wider West African grounding | adjacent thrust current; reach/brace future |
| **Javelin / throwing spear** | mobile ranged strike, pursuit, route denial; close fallback | clear throwing diagonal and finite carried set | northern Nigerian and wider regional examples | close fallback current; throw future |
| **Bow and arrows** | ranged pressure, angle control, movement punish | draw triangle, projectile line, visible quiver | Gold Coast/Ghanaian and wider regional grounding | future ranged action |
| **Sling** | mobile ranged disruption, delayed release | circular wind-up and release tangent | broadly plausible; exact Akan lineage remains open | future ranged action |
| **Shield-and-sidearm set** | guard, intercept, counter, ally cover | shield plane leads; sidearm remains visibly usable | Gold Coast shield use; northern Nigerian and Sahel construction references | guard current; intercept/counter future |
| **Club / striking staff / hammer** | impact, stagger, posture break | short heavy arc or two-handed lever | North Gold Coast/Ghana weapon and Soninke `domolo` lineages | basic melee current; stagger future |
| **Axe / hatchet** | committed chop, eventual armour or guard break | weighted head with short recovery challenge | wider West African practical/combat vocabulary; exact forms need object-level validation | basic melee current; break future |
| **Functional throwing blade** | finite ranged burst, curved approach, space denial | unmistakable release and rotating flight | documented in parts of northern Nigeria/Chad; not all similarly shaped objects were thrown | future ranged action; use conditionally |
| **Weighted casting cord** | entangle, pull, redirect, disrupt timing | loop, cast, tension, recoil | world-original Anansi-web weapon | future control action |

Western-fantasy staples may remain where they serve readable combat—sword, spear, bow, shield, axe—but their construction, carrying systems, materials, and animation should be re-grounded rather than copied from European medieval kits.

### 4.4 Spiritual and story-power conduit weapons

A conduit weapon damages or controls through Ase, Threads, emotion, sound, story, or altered meaning. It is not a decorative “ritual focus.” Its attack must be as concrete and legible as a spear thrust.

| Conduit family | Required combat function | Readable action language | Classification boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bell / gong conduit** | sound-wave story damage, reveal, interrupt, or fear/composure pressure | lift or brace → strike/ring → visible pulse through space → damped recovery | a worn passive bell is a charm; a ceremonial bell without combat action is a relic/regalia object |
| **Talking-drum conduit** | aimed or radial rhythm attacks, morale pressure, timing disruption | tension and strike pattern → travelling pulse → answer/recovery beat | a social or ceremonial drum remains an instrument unless the game authors its combat grammar |
| **Thread-bead conduit** | mark, chain, bind, transfer, or detonate stored story power | beads count or tighten → Threads connect targets → color travels or breaks | ordinary beads are clothing/charm; the weapon version needs visible charge, targeting, and expenditure |
| **Rattle / resonant vessel conduit** | area disruption, exposure, composure break, or summoned impact | shake/pour/turn → material or sound establishes an area → effect resolves | culturally specific forms and meanings require review; an inert vessel belongs with relics or consumables |
| **Story spindle / weaving conduit** | stitch wounds in reality, sever hostile Threads, bind movement, or redirect an effect | draw Thread → form line/knot → pull, cut, or release | world-original; must not invent pseudo-Adinkra symbols |

Conduits should use sound, vibration, Thread tension, ember, dust, breath, emotion color, and changes in the environment. Avoid lasers, holograms, generic spell beams, and arbitrary floating glyphs.

### 4.4a Visual and auditory grammar for conduit attacks

Conduit weapons carry the heaviest legibility burden in the catalogue: they must read as clearly as a spear thrust with no beam, glyph, or magic-circle shortcut. Build every conduit attack from the same three beats as the §4.1 admission test — anticipation, contact/effect, recovery — and give each beat both a visual and an auditory read, not just one.

**Visual — wind and path, not light.**

- Anticipation: air visibly gathers or is displaced around the object before release — dust lifts, cloth or loose thread trembles, a nearby flame bends toward or away from the source.
- Travel: the effect moves through space along a real path, not a static beam — a wind-line that bends around obstacles, a thread that pulls taut and snaps toward the target, ash or dust carried and dispersed along the route, cloth or grass disturbed in a line as the effect passes.
- Recovery: the disturbance settles rather than vanishing — dust falls, thread goes slack, cloth stops moving. A conduit attack should look like it displaced real air and left something behind, never like an overlay switched off.

**Auditory — pitch, rhythm, decay, and silence.**

- Anticipation: a rising or tightening sound cues the coming attack — the first strike building tension in a cord, breath before speech.
- Contact/effect: pitch and rhythm carry the type of effect the family table above assigns — a struck, resonant tone for damage or reveal; a broken, off-rhythm pattern for interrupt or disruption; a held, sustained tone for a bind or mark.
- Recovery: the sound decays audibly rather than cutting off — a bell's ring fading, a drumbeat's echo dying out. Where a conduit's power comes from restraint or timing (Onyamesu, Okomfo), a beat of deliberate silence before or after the sound can itself be the tell — silence is part of the vocabulary, not the absence of one.

This applies most directly to bell/gong, talking-drum, thread-bead, and story-spindle conduits above, and to any Calling whose weapon-expression bias leans on a conduit weapon (§4.7, §4.8). A concept sheet or animation pass for a conduit weapon is incomplete until it names both channels for every beat.

### 4.5 Excluded from the normal weapon catalogue

The following belong in a later relic, regalia, charm, consumable, or story-item document unless a separate combat definition passes the admission test:

- Akan/Asante state swords and close reproductions of royal `afena` or `afenatene`;
- ɔkyeame office staffs and other insignia of civic or court authority;
- prestige flywhisks, stools, crowns, linguist regalia, and processional objects;
- purely ceremonial versions of swords, throwing-knife forms, bells, drums, beads, vessels, and staffs;
- a support tool that braces, carries, or heals narratively but supplies no combat action.

An object may have two distinct game records. For example, a historically grounded bell might be a relic, while a fictional Thread-tuned bell built around an attack profile is a conduit weapon. The art, name, description, animation, and mechanics must make the distinction explicit.

### 4.6 Regalia and attribution cautions

- Do not make every curved sword an ordinary combat `afena`; documented Akan examples frequently carry courtly, oath, status, or state authority.
- Reserve close reproductions, gold-covered hilts, openwork blades, and regalia scale for story-significant objects outside the ordinary weapon pool.
- Do not turn ɔkyeame staffs into generic mage staffs.
- Do not infer combat use from an object’s silhouette or museum category alone. Some northern Nigerian/Chadian throwing blades were missiles; other similarly described forms were carried as formal costume.
- Use fictional in-world item names until Twi and other language naming receives cultural validation.

### 4.7 Calling affinity vocabulary

Until the equipment system defines numeric values, concept documents should use only these qualitative terms:

- **Resonant:** the weapon naturally reinforces the Calling’s existing behavior grammar.
- **Adaptive:** the combination is coherent but changes emphasis or requires a different posture.
- **Tension:** the combination pulls against the Calling’s instincts and should create distinctive animation or behavior—not an equip restriction.
- **Unsettled:** Uncalled use; the Echo has not yet established a Calling relationship to the weapon.

Do not turn these terms into item rarity, proficiency gates, or fixed class ownership without a separate GDD decision.

For player-facing summaries, use **Preferred** for Resonant and **Disliked** for Tension. A disliked family remains equippable and can support a strong unconventional build. It should change posture, priorities, and the kind of mastery required; it must not silently apply an identity penalty unless a later mechanics decision explicitly adds one.

### 4.8 Preferred and disliked weapon families

| Calling | Preferred weapon families | Disliked weapon families | Reason for the tension |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Okofor** | shield-and-sidearm, spear/lance, club/striking staff | knife/arm dagger, functional throwing blade | limited visible interposition and ally-cover language |
| **Onyamesu** | shield-and-sidearm, bell/gong conduit, talking-drum conduit, thread-bead conduit | axe/hatchet, long sword/sabre | solitary committed attacks can pull attention and body position away from group cohesion |
| **Aduro** | long sword/sabre, axe/hatchet, club/hammer, short spear | talking-drum conduit, thread-bead conduit | staged, procedural or multi-beat setup resists immediate commitment and breach |
| **Sum-Okwanfo** | knife/arm dagger, sling, weighted casting cord, functional throwing blade | shield-and-sidearm, club/striking staff | broad defensive planes and heavy impact silhouettes reduce concealment and withdrawal options |
| **Kra-Soro** | bow, javelin/throwing spear, spear/lance, sling | knife/arm dagger, talking-drum conduit | very close or stationary radial actions offer less route, distance, and trajectory expression |
| **Okomfo** | bell/gong conduit, thread-bead conduit, story spindle, spear/lance | axe/hatchet, club/hammer | blunt immediate commitment leaves less space for reading, marking, and procedural change |

This matrix describes instinctive affinity, not ownership. Individual skills, relics, personality, experience, or a deliberate player build may overturn it.

## 5. Uncalled

### Identity

Unresolved direction, not emptiness. The Uncalled has preferences and instincts, but no confirmed Calling language yet.

### Player-facing read

- adaptable;
- cautious experimentation;
- borrowed or practical equipment;
- ability without specialization.

### Silhouette and pose

- quiet vertical or slightly guarded silhouette;
- modest shoulder width and minimal flare;
- asymmetry comes from repairs or borrowed pieces, not ornament;
- weapon held competently but without a signature stance;
- attentive gaze, uncertain weight distribution, room to grow.

### Garment lineage

- sober sleeveless or short-sleeved cotton tunic;
- uncomplicated trousers or divided wrap-trousers;
- restrained woven edging;
- no prestige drape and no Calling-defining shoulder architecture.

### Gear language

- one repaired knee or forearm guard;
- plain leather or layered-cloth belt;
- practical sandals;
- visibly mixed origin, but not visually noisy.

### Weapon expression

The Uncalled may equip every weapon, but has no settled Calling bias and therefore no formal preferred or disliked family. Show experimentation rather than incompetence: a sling, tool-club, utility knife, bow, spear, shield, or conduit weapon may all be used, but without a signature Calling stance.

### Charm language

- one cord knot;
- one plain bead;
- a small unmarked brass or wood token.

### Avoid

- a complete color scheme;
- elaborate armor;
- prestigious cloth;
- a signature weapon that makes the Uncalled look like a hidden seventh Calling.

## 6. Okofor — Strong Ward

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true by bearing danger for others and refusing to let the line break.

### Combat grammar

Intercept → brace → redirect pressure → answer while holding.

### Silhouette and pose

- broad upper silhouette;
- low, planted center of gravity;
- strongest protection at shoulder, chest edge, forearm, and forward leg;
- shield precedes the body;
- spear line crosses or protects another person’s imagined space;
- never posed as an aggressive conqueror by default.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** fugu/batakari-derived combat smock.

- retain broad shoulders, constructed strip seams, and flared movement;
- shorten or split the lower volume for stance changes;
- pair with gathered trousers or combat shorts over fitted under-trousers;
- use narrow-strip cotton rhythm rather than generic printed geometry.

### Gear language

- quilted strip-woven shoulder mantle;
- layered cotton torso reinforcement;
- leather or basketry forearm guard;
- reinforced forward knee and shin;
- broad belt that visually transfers load into the hips.

### Weapon-expression bias

Okofor makes any weapon protective. A bow controls approach lanes; a blade becomes a countering tool; a sling interrupts pressure; a conduit weapon stabilizes or contests threatened space.

- **Preferred:** shield-and-sidearm sets, spears/lances, clubs/striking staffs.
- **Adaptive examples:** bow used for covering fire; broad blade used from a guarded counter stance.
- **Disliked:** knives/arm daggers and functional throwing blades, which offer little visible interposition or ally-cover space.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- shields may draw from documented Akan shield presence, but final full-size construction needs additional object-specific validation;
- favor warm wood, hide, woven reinforcement, and small brass fittings;
- spearheads remain functional iron shapes, not fantasy tridents;
- a side blade may borrow Akan blade proportions but should not automatically reproduce royal afena regalia.

### Charm language

- sturdy braided knot;
- small shield-form brass weight;
- linked rings suggesting duty shared;
- charm tied close to the body so it does not interfere with interposition.

### Palette and material bias

Indigo-over-yellow forest green, deep-dip indigo-black, undyed ivory cotton, camwood terracotta accents, compact brass. High visual weight; low sparkle.

### Avoid

- European tower shields;
- plate pauldrons;
- knight stance;
- oversized heroic spear posing;
- turning protection into passive immobility.

## 7. Onyamesu — Root

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true by tending life, holding communal morale together, and sustaining others through care and presence.

### Combat grammar

Stabilize → share burden → restore cohesion → keep others acting.

### Silhouette and pose

- open chest and grounded hips;
- generous, enclosing curves rather than a narrow healer silhouette;
- one arm often extends toward an ally or holds communal space;
- equipment sits close and secure, ready for repeated service;
- calm authority, never timid backline fragility.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** Ghanaian formal tailoring translated into combat structure.

- feminine versions may draw from kaba tailoring: structured blouse, shaped waist, purposeful shoulder and hip volume;
- combat bottoms reinterpret the slit through divided wrap-trousers, an open-front overskirt, or a secured split panel—never an ankle-bound formal skirt in battle;
- masculine and unisex versions use a tailored kaftan or Ghanaian formal-jacket logic with the same shaped shoulder, waist, and hip rhythm;
- this is a shared design grammar, not a claim that a historical “male kaba” exists.

### Gear language

- broad padded collar or yoke;
- layered cloth shield or medium woven shield;
- reinforced carrying straps and waist support;
- quilted hip panels that suggest burden-bearing;
- small pouches for restorative or morale-support objects.

### Weapon-expression bias

Onyamesu makes any weapon sustaining. A spear braces space beside an ally; a bow suppresses threats to cohesion; a blade is used to free, interrupt, or protect; a conduit weapon reinforces morale and continuity.

- **Preferred:** shield-and-sidearm sets, bell/gong conduits, talking-drum conduits, thread-bead conduits.
- **Adaptive examples:** bow used from a stable support position; spear used as a brace rather than a charge.
- **Disliked:** axes/hatchets and long swords/sabres, whose solitary committed attacks can pull attention and body position away from the group.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- the shield is the primary mass; the support object is secondary;
- no tall crescent staff as the default;
- bells, bowls, and vessels need culturally reviewed designs before being assigned precise ritual meanings;
- actions should look like reinforcing and carrying, not spellcasting.

### Charm language

- paired beads;
- linked cord loops;
- a tiny lidded vessel;
- soft bell or seed-pod form used with restraint.

### Palette and material bias

Undyed ivory cotton, camwood terracotta, indigo-over-yellow forest green, mid-dip mature indigo, soft antique gold leaf. A one- or two-dip soft blue may appear as emotional identity color, not as “healer magic.”

### Avoid

- generic healer robes;
- white-and-gold cleric coding;
- floating magic circles;
- medicinal stereotypes;
- reducing care to health restoration alone.

## 8. Aduro — Break

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true by meeting danger directly and creating momentum through force, courage, and decisive action.

### Combat grammar

Commit → breach → displace → exploit the opening.

### Silhouette and pose

- forward diagonal;
- compact torso with free hips and knees;
- weapon arc clearly travels through the target line;
- asymmetrical reinforcement on striking shoulder, forearm, hip, and lead knee;
- stance reads as a decision already made.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** dashiki-derived short combat tunic.

- preserve the recognizable loose body, broad neckline panel, and sleeve volume;
- do not reduce it to a fitted T-shirt with decorative trim;
- split the side seams deeply for lunging;
- pair with gathered trousers, reinforced shorts, or wrap-over combat panels.

### Gear language

- impact shoulder wrap rather than a fantasy pauldron;
- braced forearm and lead shin;
- broad wrapped waist;
- minimal back weight;
- equipment looks repaired through use rather than ceremonially pristine.

### Weapon-expression bias

Aduro makes any weapon commit. A shield drives forward; a bow fires into an opening at closer risk; a conduit weapon becomes forceful threshold pressure; a sling starts movement rather than prolonging distance.

- **Preferred:** long swords/sabres, axes/hatchets, clubs/hammers, short spears.
- **Adaptive examples:** shield used to breach; bow used for decisive opening shots rather than patient control.
- **Disliked:** talking-drum and thread-bead conduits whose staged, multi-beat setup resists immediate commitment.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- broad blades favor decisive mass and a readable forward edge;
- use restrained brass at the grip, never gold overload;
- ceremonial openwork and royal double-pommel treatment belong to high-status story items, not every drop;
- the axe must avoid Viking or medieval-European silhouettes.

### Charm language

- tight wrist beads;
- a compact brass responsibility token;
- a wrapped grip charm that records use;
- nothing long enough to trail during a committed strike.

### Palette and material bias

Camwood terracotta, ochre-to-ember orange earth pigment, indigo-over-yellow forest green, deep-dip dark indigo, raw iron, compact gold. Bright and forceful without turning red into a mandatory class color.

### Avoid

- berserker coding;
- giant swords;
- heavy plate;
- rage as the sole emotional read;
- copying an afena while treating its court meaning as irrelevant.

## 9. Sum-Okwanfo — Veil

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true through concealment, exact timing, and unseen approach, acting at the break in the weave.

### Combat grammar

Commit at the break in the weave → misdirect → strike decisively → withdraw only once the opening is spent.

### Silhouette and pose

- long broken diagonal with soft trailing layers;
- narrow profile that can turn side-on;
- hands separated by cord tension, sling rotation, or precise short-blade work;
- weight sits on the edge of movement, not in a crouched “assassin” cliché;
- face reads watchful and calculating rather than sinister.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** split kaftan or long light overlayer.

- preserve vertical flow and long front/back panels;
- open the sides and center for fast steps;
- use a quieter exterior with flashes of woven color revealed in motion;
- layer over fitted or gathered trousers;
- hidden pockets follow actual seams.

### Gear language

- flexible waist and cross-body harness;
- soft forearm protection;
- compact pouches integrated into the garment;
- quiet woven or leather foot wraps and sandals;
- very little rigid shoulder armor.

### Weapon-expression bias

Sum-Okwanfo makes any weapon indirect and timing-led. A spear attacks through an unseen angle; a shield becomes decoy and moving cover; a bow favors concealed timing; a conduit weapon marks a weakness before intervention.

- **Preferred:** knives/arm daggers, slings, weighted casting cords, functional throwing blades.
- **Adaptive examples:** spear used from flank entry; shield used to misdirect attention.
- **Disliked:** shield-and-sidearm sets and clubs/striking staffs, whose broad or heavy silhouettes reduce concealment and quick withdrawal.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- the weighted cord is explicitly fictional world design, not presented as a documented Akan weapon;
- cords use braided cotton/leather with stone, fired-clay, or brass weights;
- short blades stay practical and compact;
- no poison coding unless a separate culturally and mechanically justified system establishes it.

### Charm language

- hidden knot under the wrist;
- small pouch stitched inside a seam;
- braided thread loop;
- one bead that becomes visible only in movement.

### Palette and material bias

Indigo-over-yellow deep forest green, soot-and-earth charcoal-brown, ash-muted camwood terracotta, narrow gold-leaf flashes, a cooler blue-green from a lighter indigo overdye pass. Keep the Calling vibrant and mystical, never murky or villain-coded.

### Avoid

- black hood;
- assassin leathers;
- knives everywhere;
- smoke effects as the only concealment metaphor;
- making Veil morally suspicious by default.

## 10. Kra-Soro — Path

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true by reading path, distance, angle, and changing ground.

### Combat grammar

Read route → claim angle → warn or reposition → punish movement.

### Silhouette and pose

- strongest horizontal and diagonal reach in the set;
- open shoulders and extended sightline;
- asymmetry comes from quiver, throwing set, map pouch, or field wrap;
- one foot already selects the next route;
- head and weapon point toward different information when scouting.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** tailored short coat or compact kaftan-derived field suit.

- clean vertical front opening;
- sleeves cut for unrestricted draw or throw;
- short divided lower panels or tailored shorts over fitted under-trousers;
- use strip-woven or narrow-striped cloth strategically, not as full visual noise;
- include a headwrap or practical field cap as a variant, not a mandatory stereotype.

### Gear language

- light bracer on the bow arm;
- quiver or javelin carrier integrated with shoulder and hip balance;
- reinforced sandals and lower-leg wraps;
- small field pouch;
- minimal chest bulk so the weapon arc stays readable.

### Weapon-expression bias

Kra-Soro makes any weapon spatial. A shield controls a route; a blade punishes movement through an angle; a conduit weapon reads terrain thresholds; a spear or bow makes trajectories explicit.

- **Preferred:** bows, javelins/throwing spears, spears/lances, slings.
- **Adaptive examples:** broad blade used for movement punish; shield set used to close or preserve a path.
- **Disliked:** knives/arm daggers and talking-drum conduits, whose very close or stationary radial actions offer less route, distance, and trajectory expression.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- use wood, cane, fibre binding, iron arrowheads, leather/skin-covered quiver construction, and woven straps;
- quivers may carry restrained cowrie or bead detail where appropriate, but avoid decorative excess;
- no English longbow silhouette and no fantasy recurved ornament by default;
- bows and arrows are historically grounded in Ghana and the wider region, but exact ethnic attribution of individual museum objects must remain explicit.

### Charm language

- cord-wrapped stone;
- small whistle;
- bead counter;
- compact brass disk used as a personal marker, not an invented compass symbol.

### Palette and material bias

Undyed ivory cotton, deep-dip indigo, a brighter leaf green from a higher-yellow indigo overdye, one-dip soft blue, golden ochre earth pigment, dark unfinished wood. High daylight readability and strong material contrast.

### Avoid

- generic woodland ranger;
- green hood and leather armor cliché;
- European archery kit;
- feathers used as shorthand for “tribal scout”;
- confusing field-reading with Okomfo’s omen-reading.

## 11. Okomfo — Rite

### Identity

The Echo who becomes true by reading spirit, sign, and hidden pattern, then acting from deeper understanding.

### Combat grammar

Interpret → reveal → mark a threshold → reshape the meaning of the moment.

### Silhouette and pose

- strong continuous cloth arc;
- grounded upright torso;
- compact ritual object held where its action can be seen;
- hands articulate listening, marking, ringing, pouring, or cutting a threshold;
- authority comes from attention and procedure, not spectacle.

### Garment lineage

**Primary:** combat-secured ntoma/ntama over a simple underlayer.

- the outer cloth must remain one continuous drape;
- secure at shoulder and waist while preserving a large diagonal fold;
- free the weapon arm and lower legs;
- use actual strip rhythm and cloth scale rather than two separate decorative scarves;
- reserve the fullest prestige drape for non-combat, rite, or high-Standing scenes.

### Gear language

- narrow hidden shoulder anchor under the drape;
- wrapped waist support;
- small ritual pouch or vessel carried close;
- bracer only where the drape exposes the arm;
- restrained sandals and no conventional fantasy armor silhouette.

### Weapon-expression bias

Okomfo makes any weapon interpretive. A bow tests an omen-marked line; a spear defines a threshold; a shield becomes a bounded ritual surface; a blade severs a marked relation rather than merely dealing damage.

- **Preferred:** bell/gong conduits, thread-bead conduits, story spindles, spears/lances.
- **Adaptive examples:** bow used after revelation; spear planted or carried to define a threshold before striking.
- **Disliked:** axes/hatchets and clubs/hammers, whose blunt immediate commitment leaves less space for reading, marking, and procedure.

These are behavior biases, not equipment permissions.

### Weapon depiction rules

- a tall office or ritual staff is regalia, not a default weapon; a combat staff must be mechanically and visually constructed for striking;
- office staffs and state swords carry specific social meanings and must not be borrowed as generic “magic gear”;
- bells, drums, beads, vessels, white clay, and shrine-linked objects require Ghanaian/Akan cultural review before final names, symbols, and ritual animations are locked;
- if such an object occupies the weapon slot, its targeting, damaging or controlling effect, cost, telegraph, and counterplay must be explicit;
- effects should travel through sound, ash/kaolin-like marks, ember, thread tension, and changed space—not beams or holograms.

### Charm language

- small bell;
- wrapped spindle;
- lidded vessel;
- white bead or pale clay accent;
- tightly controlled brass and cord assembly.

### Palette and material bias

Undyed ivory cotton, indigo-over-yellow deep forest green, kaolin clay (hyire) white and pale-clay accents — the same white used in Akan purification and healing practice, sacred rather than decorative — restrained ancestral gold leaf, camwood ember-terracotta, and a selective emotion color layered on top per §3.2. Sacred and luminous rather than dark.

### Avoid

- wizard robes;
- crescent staff as a generic fantasy shortcut;
- invented sigils;
- purple magic;
- spectacle overpowering interpretation;
- conflating Okomfo with Kra-Soro’s practical field awareness.

## 12. Cross-Calling Readability Matrix

| Calling | Dominant body shape | Calling-led action attitude | Armor emphasis | Weapon-independent identity anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uncalled | quiet vertical | exploratory, not yet settled | isolated repair | sober garment and unresolved stance |
| Okofor | broad block / low triangle | interpose before answering | shoulder, forearm, lead leg | fugu-derived breadth and protective body placement |
| Onyamesu | enclosing oval / stable column | sustain contact and cohesion | collar, torso edge, hips | tailored enclosing volume and open supporting gesture |
| Aduro | forward wedge | commit through the opening | striking side, lead knee | dashiki-derived mobility and forward weight |
| Sum-Okwanfo | broken diagonal | delay, misdirect, enter precisely | waist, forearm, light foot | split-kaftan flow and low-profile asymmetry |
| Kra-Soro | long horizontal / open diagonal | read and control routes | weapon-side arm, lower leg | tailored field silhouette and spatial gaze |
| Okomfo | continuous draped diagonal | interpret before changing the threshold | hidden anchors under cloth | continuous ntoma/ntama drape and procedural hands |

### Silhouette test

A concept passes only if a grayscale silhouette can still distinguish at least five of the seven identities without seeing facial markings, color, or surface pattern.

### Concept scale versus game-ready scale

The test above describes full concept-sheet legibility. That is necessary but not sufficient: a player meets most Echoes as a small portrait or a battle-grid token, not a full-body render. Two pairs in the current identity set are already distinct on paper but sit close enough at small scale to need deliberate reinforcement when they move from concept art to game-ready assets:

- **Onyamesu and Okomfo** — Onyamesu's tailored, structured volume (§7 Garment lineage) and Okomfo's single continuous draped cloth (§11 Garment lineage) are different constructions, but the matrix above currently differentiates them by a hand gesture (open supporting gesture vs. procedural hands) — a detail that is often the first thing lost when a character shrinks to icon size.
- **Kra-Soro and Sum-Okwanfo** — Kra-Soro's short, fitted field coat and Sum-Okwanfo's long, trailing overlayer are different garments, but both read as a diagonal-leaning silhouette once reduced.

For these pairs, the garment-length and structure contrast already written into each Calling's Garment lineage must be exaggerated in the pose itself — Onyamesu's enclosed, blocky mass against Okomfo's open diagonal drape line; Kra-Soro's short/compact shape against Sum-Okwanfo's long/trailing one — rather than relying on a hand gesture or a few degrees of angle to carry the difference alone. Any game-ready asset pass (portrait, battle token, or icon) must re-run the silhouette test at actual in-game display size, not only at concept-sheet size.

### Mixed-loadout test

A concept passes only if:

1. swapping two adjacent Callings’ weapons does not erase their garment and posture identity;
2. the new weapon visibly changes action profile;
3. the character still looks like the same person and Calling;
4. no Calling becomes visually invalid because it lacks its “correct” item.

### Same-weapon / different-Calling test

Every weapon family should receive at least one concept sheet showing all six Callings using the same base item. The object remains recognizable; the relationship to it changes.

Example using one short spear:

| Calling | How the same spear is expressed |
|---|---|
| Okofor | crosses the threat line, braces, and intercepts |
| Onyamesu | stays close to allies, supports a burden, and creates safe space |
| Aduro | shortens the grip, commits forward, and breaks formation |
| Sum-Okwanfo | holds close to the body, enters from an oblique angle, and withdraws |
| Kra-Soro | measures distance, changes lanes, and throws or pursues |
| Okomfo | marks a boundary, reads the charged line, then acts at the threshold |

Example using one bow:

| Calling | How the same bow is expressed |
|---|---|
| Okofor | covering fire that protects an ally’s approach or retreat |
| Onyamesu | stable suppression and threat removal around the group |
| Aduro | forceful opening shot followed by immediate forward movement |
| Sum-Okwanfo | concealed timing, misdirection, and firing from the weak angle |
| Kra-Soro | sustained route, distance, and angle mastery |
| Okomfo | patient shot following omen, mark, or hidden-state revelation |

Future Calling concept sheets should include:

1. one neutral full-body silhouette;

4. front and rear gear-placement study;

The Calling sheet is a character-and-gameplay document, not an item catalogue. Items should be shown in use.

## 14. Cultural Validation Boundaries

The following are safe as broad concept foundations but are not final cultural approval:

- use of Ghanaian and Akan garment lineages in a fictional combat setting;
- use of historically present broad weapon categories;
- world-original weighted cords and story-power conduit weapons;
- adaptation of cloth and regalia into game-readable silhouettes.

The following require consultation with a Ghanaian/Akan cultural historian, practitioner, textile specialist, or appropriate maker before ship:

- final Twi item names;
- priestly/Okomfo objects, gestures, body markings, ritual sequences, and shrine associations;
- exact meanings assigned to bells, vessels, flywhisks, clay, beads, and colors;
- reproduction of royal or chiefly regalia;
- use of named kente, adinkra, or other encoded patterns;
- distinctions among Asante, Fante, Akuapem, Bono, Nzema, Akyem, and other Akan traditions;
- use of northern Ghanaian garment lineages within an Akan-centered world without acknowledging their origins and exchange.

The creative rule is not “avoid specificity.” It is “label what is documented, what is adapted, and what is invented.”

## 15. Research Notes and Sources

These references establish historical object and garment vocabulary. They do not independently validate the fictional Calling-to-object assignments above.

### Weapons and regalia

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Ɔkyeame poma with stool, chain, and crossed swords](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/315887): distinguishes royal spokesperson staffs and describes afena as major Akan royal regalia and ritual weapons.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Sword gold weight](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/317679): identifies the depicted afena and its courtly/regalia context.
- Smithsonian National Museum of African Art, [State sword](https://africa.si.edu/collection/object/nmafa_2015-16-1): describes state swords as chiefly regalia used in installation, procession, messaging, and funerary display.
- British Museum, [State sword and sheath](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1900-0427-6-a-b): documents an Asante afena’s iron, wood, gold, skin, cane, and leather construction.
- British Museum, [Ceremonial afenatene](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1896-0519-4): identifies a large Asante sword as primarily display and authority regalia.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Akan shield gold weight](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/317681): confirms the shield in Akan material imagery.
- John K. Thornton, [Warfare in Atlantic Africa, 1500–1800](https://marxistnkrumaistforum.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/thornton-warfare-in-atlantic-africa-1500e280931800.pdf): notes early Gold Coast infantry use of swords, lances, bows, and shields.
- British Museum, [Ghana quiver](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1978-22-758-a-b): documents wood, skin, woven strap, wool, and cowrie construction for a quiver collected in Ghana.
- British Museum, [Dagomba arrow collected through the 1817 Asante embassy](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1818-1114-15): documents cane, iron, and fibre arrow construction while making its northern Ghanaian attribution explicit.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Udamalore plaque](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316590): distinguishes a ceremonial ivory sword from the depicted practical `uda` raised for combat, demonstrating why use must be researched separately from silhouette.
- British Museum, [Throwing spear from northern Nigeria](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1935-1110-32): documents an iron-headed, counterpoised throwing spear on a bamboo shaft.
- British Museum, [Fulbe spears from the Upper Benue](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1856-0218-2-a-f): documents regional iron-headed spear construction.
- British Museum, [Weapon from northern Gold Coast or northern Nigeria](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1950-40-5): documents a wood-and-iron striking weapon or hammer.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Soninke domolo](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/314915): documents a staff serving as weapon, practical tool, and ritual object, with an integrated sheathed knife.
- British Museum, [Hausa arm dagger](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1983-11-17-a): documents a straight iron blade, leather-covered wood hilt, and crescent pommel from northern Nigeria.
- British Museum, [Tiv palm-loop knife](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1911-155): documents a compact iron knife with a palm loop and records a broader Tiv weapon vocabulary.
- British Museum, [Manding sword and scabbard](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1979-01-4677-b): documents a curved single-edged sword and tooled leather scabbard from the western savannah/Gambia region.
- British Museum, [Tuareg-style takouba from Sokoto](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1944-06-4-a): documents a straight sword lineage from northern Nigeria.
- British Museum, [Sara-type throwing knife](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1926-1015-7): documents a missile form found across Chad, northern Nigeria, and northern Cameroon.
- British Museum, [Margi formal knife](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1924-1217-30): documents a similarly categorized form that was not used as a missile, reinforcing the need for object-specific functional validation.
- British Museum, [Tangale or Tula shield](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1932-0608-38): documents hide construction, a conical boss, and a sewn wooden ring from northern Nigeria.

### Conduit-object references

- Smithsonian National Museum of African Art, [Yoruba ìyáàlù dùndún talking drum](https://www.si.edu/object/iyalu-dundun-drum%3Anmafa_74-9-14): documents a speech-mimicking tension drum and its material construction; the game’s combat application remains fictional.
- British Museum, [Yoruba bell](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af1999-01-48): documents a culturally specific bell whose original religious identity must not be reassigned casually.
- Princeton University Art Museum, [Yoruba agogo](https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/art/collections/objects/59993): provides a further material reference for a struck metal sound object, not proof of historical weapon use.

### Textiles and garments

- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Royal man’s kente](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/894795): documents Asante narrow-strip construction, materials, prestige, and structured pattern rhythm.
- Smithsonian National Museum of African Art, [Asante wrapper](https://africa.si.edu/collection/object/nmafa_EJ10571): documents strip assembly and named pattern structures in an Asante silk wrapper.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, [Man’s Prestige Cloth](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/320678): documents the scale and over-shoulder draping of Asante prestige cloth.
- Ghana National Commission on Culture, [Weaving Identity in the North — The Smock](https://ghanaculture.gov.gh/fashion-as-living-heritage-2/): describes fugu/batakari construction, broad/flared cut, mobility, and northern Ghanaian lineage.
- Kuma-Kpobee, Power, and Otieno, [Utilisation of Traditional Dress: The Case of Slit and Kaba in Ghana](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/13851/): describes kaba, slit, and the accompanying cloth and their social use in Ghana.
- Christopher Richards, [“The Models for Africa”: Accra’s Independence-Era Fashion Culture](https://direct.mit.edu/afar/article/49/3/8/54920/The-Models-for-Africa-Accra-s-Independence-Era): discusses Ghanaian local fashion, kaba construction, kente tailoring, and modern design transformation.

### Pigments and dyes

- Shepherd Textiles, [Dyeing with Camwood (Baphia nitida)](https://shepherdtextiles.com/dyeing-with-camwood): documents camwood's insoluble red-brown pigments and traditional dye extraction.
- PlantUse/PROTA, [Baphia nitida](https://uses.plantnet-project.org/en/Baphia_pubescens_(PROTA)): documents camwood body-paint use across Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, and Ghana, including its specific use by Ghanaian warriors marking combat and ritual dance.
- Adire African Textiles, [Indigo in West Africa](https://www.adireafricantextiles.com/textiles-resources-sub-saharan-africa/an-introduction-to-sub-saharan-african-textiles/indigo-in-west-africa/): documents the West African indigo-dyeing tradition.
- Timothy S. Y. Lam Museum of Anthropology, [Nigeria: Make a Yoruba Adire Cloth](https://lammuseum.wfu.edu/2020/07/nigeria-make-a-yoruba-adire-cloth/): documents indigo dye preparation and the relationship between dip count and final color depth (twenty-plus dips for a deep blue-black).
- “Color as a Cultural Resilience Strategy: Material-Semiotic Systems in Ghana's Indigenous Spirituality” (ResearchGate): documents white clay (hyire) use in Akan healing and purification ritual, applied to the forehead and eyelids.

These establish real pigment and dye behavior. They do not establish specific ritual or clan meaning for any color in this document — see §14.

## 16. Current Direction to Lock for the Next Visual Pass

The next visual pass should not assign one canonical weapon to each Calling. It should be a controlled two-sheet study:

### Sheet A — Calling identity without weapon ownership

- Uncalled plus all six Callings in neutral or lightly equipped poses;
- garment, armor distribution, posture, and emotional presence carry the identity;
- weapons are absent or visually secondary;
- grayscale silhouettes must distinguish at least five identities.

### Sheet B — one weapon across all Callings

- choose one weapon family, initially **short spear** or **bow**;
- show all six Callings using the exact same base item;
- vary grip, stance, distance, timing, gaze, and relationship to allies or space;
- demonstrate that Calling changes expression while weapon changes capability;
- include Uncalled as an unsettled control case if layout permits.

After those pass, create the full mixed-loadout sheet using freely exchanged weapons.

Across all sheets:

- every garment must retain its lineage after armor is added;
- charms remain worn, small, and emotionally meaningful;
- no Calling is reduced to a staff silhouette, a signature weapon, or a color code;
- unusual Calling-and-weapon combinations must look intentional and playable;
- preferred and disliked weapon bias remains soft and never becomes an equip restriction;
- every displayed weapon must show a credible combat grip, anticipation, effect or contact moment, and recovery—not only a catalogue pose;
- regalia and passive ceremonial objects remain outside the weapon sheet;
- conduit weapons must visualize their target, damage or control effect, cost/timing, and counterplay as clearly as physical weapons.
