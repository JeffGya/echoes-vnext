# Realm Concept Art Direction

> **Status:** Working visual canon for the eleven Realm concept pages.
> **Primary design canon:** `docs/Echoes vNext Working GDD.md`.
> **Art-direction canon:** `docs/art-direction.md`.

## Purpose

Create one full-page environment concept for each of the ten virtue Realms and one dedicated starting Realm. Every page should establish the Realm as a distinct place with a wide environmental view and the mandatory characteristics needed to preserve its story, geography, traversal logic, objectives, and visual identity.

These are environment and concept-art pages. They are not implementation mockups, combat-board specifications, or promises that every depicted element already exists in the game.

## Locked visual language for all Realm pages

- Adult West African illustrated storybook, grounded in the project's established material culture and Anansi story-world.
- Transparent watercolor washes, matte gouache-like color blocks, and confident dark-brown hand-ink contours.
- Exaggerated shape design must come before surface rendering.
- Floating plateaus use broad, readable tops, tapered hanging silhouettes, and rhythmic web or thread arcs.
- Architecture uses compressed, hand-shaped proportions, oversized roof masses, narrow openings, and visibly simplified construction.
- Trees use calligraphic trunks and roots with a few large overlapping canopy masses.
- Terrain uses large calm color fields, sparse graphic plants, and a few oversized functional forms rather than dense rubble or repeated stones.
- Depth is compressed into a small number of theatrical layers. Gently impossible perspective is welcome when it improves clarity and rhythm.
- Pale fog and watercolor cloud washes may dissolve the outer edges of the page and floating landforms.
- Realm palettes should remain vibrant enough to carry identity. Watercolor does not mean uniformly muted or sepia.

### Avoid

- Realistically proportioned geography with a storybook filter painted over it.
- Naturalistic cliffs, realistic forests, botanical detail, or conventional landscape perspective.
- Dense micro-stones, granular sand, repeated pebbles, excessive crosshatching, or incidental surface noise.
- Mosaic-like watercolor blotting, tiled blooms, cauliflower edges, or large repeated pigment patches.
- Generic Western fantasy, Gothic ruins, cathedrals, castles, or ornamental pseudo-African motifs.
- Treating golden webs as decoration without a structural or narrative purpose.

## Page structure

Each Realm receives one landscape page:

1. A large wide environment shot occupying most of the page.
2. A restrained row of isolated studies for the Realm's mandatory characteristics.
3. A Realm title and a short objective or thematic descriptor.
4. No gameplay arrows, projected routes, footprints, grids, or interface overlays unless a later brief explicitly asks for them.
5. Characters and inhabitants are optional and secondary. Environment, story function, and Realm identity remain primary.

# 01 — Starting Realm

## Working title

**The Frayed Crossing**

The name is a working title, not yet locked. It expresses passage, damaged connection, and the wider story-and-weaving theme without framing the opening as a catastrophic wound.

## Narrative purpose

The starting Realm is the protected ember-recovery mini-trial from the opening sequence. It directly connects the starter Echo's first danger to the restoration of the dormant Sanctum:

1. Leave the dormant Sanctum.
2. Survive the protected first battle.
3. Recover the bound ember.
4. Carry the ember home.
5. Return it to the Ase hearth and bring the Sanctum back to life.

The environment must make this entire cause-and-effect chain understandable without relying on explanatory text.

## Spatial sequence

### 1. Ordinary Sanctum gate

The starting Realm does **not** use the formal Realm gate used for later Realm travel.

An ordinary physical gate stands at the edge of the Sanctum. It belongs to the Sanctum's earthen and thatched architecture and opens onto the first bridge. It must not read as a magical portal, rupture, or separate dimension.

### 2. Bridge from the Sanctum

A regular traversable bridge leads from the gate toward the nearby trial islands. Woven fiber construction and supporting web-lines connect it to the project's larger thread language, but it remains a physical bridge that the Echo can cross.

The dormant Sanctum should remain visible behind or beyond the gate so the starting point and eventual destination are never ambiguous.

### 3. First island — battle area

The first floating island is the protected combat space.

- It is broad enough to read as an arena rather than a bridge encounter.
- It uses the normal Realm terrain language in reduced complexity.
- Walkable ground, obstruction, void, unstable surface, and thread-based binding or hazards remain visually separate.
- The combat space is the first destination after leaving the Sanctum; the bound ember is not collected here.

### 4. Second island — bound ember

The second principal island contains the bound ember.

- The ember is small, portable, and clearly different from a shrine, relic monument, bonfire, or Ase hearth.
- It may rest in a shallow clay vessel or low woven cradle.
- A corrupted knot or binding can communicate why the ember must be recovered without turning the objective into a large fantasy structure.
- This island should feel like a recovery space reached after the first battle, not a second arena of equal visual weight.

### 5. Return traversal

After collecting the ember, the Echo traverses bridges back across the floating fragments and returns through the ordinary gate.

The wide shot should show enough of the returning bridge network for the journey home to be spatially plausible. It does not need path arrows or footprints; the arrangement of islands and crossings should communicate it naturally.

### 6. Return to the Ase flame

The Echo carries the recovered ember back into the dormant Sanctum and returns it to the cold Ase hearth. The ember reawakens the Ase Flame and visibly brings the Sanctum back to life.

The Realm page should foreshadow this destination through the visible Sanctum connection. The actual relighting occurs in the Sanctum, not on either trial island.

## World continuity

The starting Realm is a nearby outer fragment of the Sanctum's floating-island world, not a sudden new forest, terrestrial plain, or unrelated biome.

It shares the dormant Sanctum's:

- ochre earth and hand-shaped stone;
- sparse broad-leaf plants;
- clay vessels and woven fiber construction;
- deep greens, warm terracottas, pale blue sky, ivory fog, and selective Akan-gold threads;
- floating plateaus connected and supported by web-like story threads.

The trial may feel less inhabited and less settled than the house, but it must remain visibly part of the same place.

## Mandatory elements for the concept page

- Ordinary Sanctum gate.
- Physical bridge leading away from the Sanctum.
- First floating island as the protected battle area.
- Second floating island as the bound-ember recovery area.
- Additional bridge traversal that makes the return journey plausible.
- Dormant Sanctum visible as the origin and destination.
- Small portable bound ember.
- Clear floating-island void and supporting web/thread structure.
- Exaggerated storybook proportions and watercolor/gouache finish.
